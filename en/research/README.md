# Research Series: Reliable Long-Horizon Agents (7 manuscripts)

> Author: Chenglu Yuan (袁成路), Independent Researcher, Wuhan, China · Last updated: 2026-10-08
> **Status: method-and-protocol drafts.** Seven manuscripts are complete, including internal structure checks. No comparative experiments have been run, and no performance results are reported. Each manuscript provides a testable method specification, falsifiable hypotheses, conditional properties with explicit limits, and a comparative evaluation protocol, paired with an *Evidence Check & Experiment Execution Note*.

This series studies one shared problem: **how to make long-horizon agent mechanisms falsifiable and auditable** — covering plan revision under changing requirements (01), extension-architecture boundaries (02), memory governance (03), post-action verification and structured reflection (04), claim–evidence linkage and completion claims (05), longitudinal education collaboration (06), and joint governance of constraints, epistemic state, and feedback (07).

> **What this is:** a coherent research program of falsifiable, reproducible protocols, written before the experiments so the experiments cannot quietly move the goalposts.
> **What this is not:** empirical papers, peer-reviewed results, or evidence of improved performance. Do not cite it for measured effects.

<summary>01 · Dependency-Aware Plan Revision for Coding Agents under Changing Requirements: Method, Properties, and an Evaluation Protocol</summary>

Coding agents may receive revised user requirements while carrying out a multi-step task. Updating the current step does not necessarily align downstream implementation, tests, and delivery artifacts with the new requirements, whereas complete replanning may revisit work that remains valid. This paper examines how to identify the scope of a requirement change and keep plan revision consistent with execution evidence while controlling the underlying language model and tool environment. We propose a dependency-aware revision method that links requirements, plan steps, artifacts, and acceptance evidence. The method combines impact-set computation, evidence invalidation, local review, and version-consistency checks. We establish a conditional coverage property for impact closure on a fixed graph, explicitly separating graph-level coverage from semantic correctness and from the completeness of model-generated dependencies. We also specify an evaluation protocol with four primary conditions, matched-budget controls, and component ablations. The protocol measures task success, missed impacts, downstream constraint violations, rework, and execution cost, using both shared checkpoints and end-to-end runs to distinguish revision effects from initial planning differences. No comparative agent experiments have yet been conducted for this manuscript. Its present contribution is a testable method specification and evaluation protocol, rather than evidence of improved performance. The proposed study is designed to determine when local revision is useful, when its overhead outweighs its benefits, and when broader replanning is necessary.

</details>

<summary>02 · Architectural Boundaries of Coding-Agent Extensions: A Comparative Research Protocol for Capabilities, Authority, and Portability</summary>

Coding agents can be extended through skill files, lifecycle callbacks, tool protocols, and delegated agents. Similar names across platforms may conceal different loading, execution, and authorization semantics. Consequently, feature-presence tables provide limited evidence about whether an extension can be migrated, where authority is enforced, or how failures are contained. This paper specifies a comparative research protocol centered on task contracts and execution boundaries. The protocol separately codes discovery, loading, invocation, authorization, persistence, and recovery, and attaches an explicit evidence level to every observation. Official documentation is used to define a candidate scope covering Claude Code, Codex CLI, OpenCode, and Hermes Agent, without treating documented support as runtime validation or as a platform ranking. The proposed experiments implement minimal extensions against shared behavioral contracts, measure configuration conformance and observed task outcomes, and use controlled fault injection to locate enforcement boundaries. Portability is evaluated through successful preservation of behavior and restrictions, together with measured adaptation effort, rather than through file-format similarity alone. No pinned-version cross-platform execution study has yet been conducted. The present contribution is a reproducible comparison framework and study design; claims about architectural superiority, security, or lower migration cost remain open empirical questions. The protocol also specifies how unavailable source code, unsupported contracts, version drift, and unsuccessful migrations should be reported.

</details>

<summary>03 · Agent Memory Governance under Temporal and Source Constraints: Conflict Preservation, Context Selection, and an Evaluation Protocol</summary>

Long-running agents reuse historical information, yet a relevant memory may no longer be applicable. Late-arriving records can describe obsolete states, newly recorded policies may take effect in the future, and different sources may make incompatible claims about the same subject. Under a limited context budget, selecting a single highly relevant record can also remove the update or disagreement needed to interpret it. This paper studies how temporal applicability, source rules, and unresolved conflicts can jointly govern memory updates and context selection while keeping the underlying language model fixed. We specify a method that retains source records, distinguishes observation time from effective time, resolves supersession within explicit scopes, and treats unresolved conflicts as indivisible context bundles. We state a conditional exclusion property: correctly marked invalid records cannot enter the current-fact channel when the declared eligibility rules are enforced. This property does not establish semantic truth or guarantee that conflicts have been detected. The proposed evaluation compares strong temporal-memory and retrieval-filtering controls, includes recent closely related governance methods, and separates representation changes from decision rules through controlled ablations. Outcomes include answer correctness, stale-information misuse, erroneous withholding, abstention, and lifecycle costs covering ingestion as well as retrieval and generation. No comparative experiments have been completed for this manuscript. Its contribution is a testable specification and evaluation protocol for interacting temporal, source, and budget constraints, rather than a demonstrated performance improvement or a claim of priority over existing memory systems.

</details>

<summary>04 · Post-Action Verification and Structured Reflection for Coding Agents: Separating Error Correction, Error Recurrence, and Computational Budget</summary>

A coding agent may produce a plausible explanation of failure without repairing the underlying artifact. Verification supplies observable feedback, whereas reflection attempts to convert feedback into guidance for subsequent actions. Because both interventions consume additional computation, their effects are easily confounded. This paper asks whether structured reflection provides incremental value beyond external verification and ordinary retry when the base model, initial artifact, and resource budget are controlled. We specify a reflection record comprising observed facts, causal hypotheses, discriminating checks, minimal corrections, and applicability boundaries. A fixed-size buffer carries these records into subsequent attempts without introducing a learned retrieval system. The proposed evaluation separates error detection, causal attribution, artifact repair, and recurrence on related tasks. A two-factor design crosses verification with reflection and includes both initially incorrect and initially correct candidates. Hidden acceptance tests, blinded assessment, matched-budget retries, and near-transfer tasks jointly measure benefit, harm, and cost. We derive a transition-based decomposition of net correction gain and a conditional non-degradation property for a fixed set of checked predicates. Neither establishes general model correctness or guarantees improvement on unseen tests. No comparative experiments have been completed for this manuscript. The present contribution is a testable method specification and evaluation protocol intended to distinguish behavioral correction from persuasive explanation, additional computation, and task-specific overfitting.

</details>

<summary>05 · Auditable Conclusions and Completion Claims in Tool-Using Agents: A Claim–Evidence Linking Method and Evaluation Protocol</summary>

Tool-using agents report both factual conclusions and the completion of requested work. However, the presence of a source, its support for a claim, the truth of its contents, and the actual completion of a task are distinct properties. Citations, successful tool returns, and retained execution logs cannot by themselves establish all four. This paper proposes a claim–evidence linking method for externally delivered agent reports. The method decomposes reports into independently assessable claims and associates each claim with evidence spans, source versions, execution records, acceptance conditions, and explicit limits on what can be inferred. Missing evidence, unsupported inferences, conflicting sources, stale states, and unmet completion conditions lead to different reporting decisions, including qualified statements and abstention. We analyze conditional traceability and release constraints while making clear that hashes and signatures do not establish semantic truth. The planned evaluation compares strong retrieval-and-citation systems, citation correction, structured execution reporting, and the complete method under matched resource budgets. Outcomes jointly assess factual accuracy, coverage of required information, false completion claims, audit effort, and execution costs. Adversarial conditions include irrelevant or fabricated evidence, outdated observations, and technically successful calls that fail business-level acceptance. No comparative experiments have been conducted. The present contribution is a falsifiable method specification and evaluation protocol, with effectiveness and research novelty remaining open empirical questions.

</details>

<summary>06 · Longitudinal Human–AI Collaboration for Learner Autonomy: Goal Continuity, Fading Assistance, and Unaided Transfer Evaluation</summary>

Educational AI can help learners complete immediate exercises, but performance with assistance does not establish what learners can do after support is removed. Longitudinal collaboration also raises questions about changing goals, outdated learner assessments, and reduced opportunities for independent attempts. This paper proposes a collaboration protocol that separately records learning goals, unaided performance, assistance levels, and learner choices. It maintains explicit goal continuity across sessions while using brief unaided checks to guide the gradual removal of assistance, with support restoration remaining available. Conversation length, usage frequency, and satisfaction are not treated as substitutes for learning. The proposed contribution is a testable integration and evaluation protocol, rather than the invention of fading or self-regulated learning. We specify an adult-first research sequence and a factorial comparison of goal continuity and fading assistance, supplemented by an active non-AI condition. Instructional time and access to learning resources are controlled, and the primary outcome is unaided transfer four weeks after the intervention. Secondary outcomes include immediate learning, independent strategy selection, appropriate help seeking, withdrawal, and instructor workload. The protocol addresses classroom clustering, instructor effects, missing outcomes, and the learning effects of repeated testing. Research involving minors requires a separate ethical review, parental consent, and the child's continuing assent. No participants have been recruited and no empirical effects are reported. The present manuscript provides a falsifiable protocol whose educational value and suitability for school deployment remain to be established.

</details>

<summary>07 · Joint Governance of Constraints, Epistemic State, and Feedback Calibration: A Compositional Framework and Interaction Evaluation Protocol for Tool-Using Agents</summary>

Tool-using agents can combine behavioral constraints, information checking, and feedback-based revision, yet individually reasonable components do not necessarily produce a more reliable system. Repeated checks may exhaust a shared budget, unsupported reflections may become accepted facts, and conservative rules may block otherwise solvable tasks. This paper specifies a three-layer governance framework comprising observable constraints, explicit epistemic states, and feedback calibration. Normative requirements are translated into executable conditions, supported information is separated from unresolved hypotheses, and feedback is restricted to declared state transitions. The research target is interaction among components, including complementarity, interference, cost, and error attribution, rather than the invention of layered control, evidence records, or reflection. We state a limited execution property under fixed policies, complete interception, and consistent versions, while distinguishing policy enforcement from task success and factual correctness. The proposed evaluation uses a three-factor, eight-condition design with matched models, tools, basic permissions, and lifecycle budgets. Shared-checkpoint experiments isolate local effects, whereas end-to-end experiments measure the consequences of diverging execution paths. Outcomes include joint task success, erroneous blocking, unsupported actions, calibration, and total computational cost. Primary interaction contrasts, family-level paired analysis, failure accounting, and safeguards against memory leakage are specified before results are available. No comparative experiments have been conducted for this manuscript. Its contribution is a testable interface specification and interaction protocol; cultural and philosophical motivations are not treated as evidence of an implemented cognitive mechanism or a performance improvement.

</details>
